# Web Presence Page Patterns

Use these patterns as starting structures. Adapt them to the real product,
audience, assets, and conversion goal.

## Official Or Product Site

Goal: make the visitor understand what this is, why it matters, why it is
credible, and what to do next.

Default section order:

1. first viewport: brand/product signal, precise promise, primary CTA, product
   image or real interface;
2. trust bar: logos, usage numbers, press, founder/customer proof;
3. problem reframing: what is broken in the old way;
4. product mechanism: how the product changes the user's workflow;
5. proof: screenshots, metrics, demos, comparison, security or performance;
6. use cases: 3-5 real scenarios, not generic feature cards;
7. pricing or adoption path when useful;
8. FAQ and final CTA.

## Course Landing Page

Goal: make the visitor believe the course will change their ability, not merely
consume their attention.

Default section order:

1. first viewport: course promise, target learner, outcome, teacher signal;
2. pain and aspiration: what the learner is stuck on and what becomes possible;
3. curriculum map: modules, learning progression, practice outputs;
4. proof: teacher credentials, student results, sample lesson, project gallery;
5. learning experience: format, schedule, community, feedback path;
6. offer details: price, bonus, guarantee or refund terms if real;
7. objections: time, level, prerequisites, support;
8. enrollment CTA.

Avoid: vague "master X" claims without concrete exercises or outcomes.

## Customer Case Study Page

Goal: make proof feel specific enough that a buyer can map it to their own
situation.

Default section order:

1. headline: customer, result, and the transformation in one sentence;
2. snapshot: industry, company size, challenge, solution, timeline;
3. before: what was expensive, slow, risky, or unclear;
4. intervention: what changed in workflow, system, product, or behavior;
5. results: numbers, qualitative proof, screenshots, customer quotes;
6. implementation story: decisions, tradeoffs, adoption path;
7. why it worked: principles that generalize;
8. next case or CTA.

Avoid: making case studies look like testimonials. A case study must contain
causality: problem -> change -> result -> transferable lesson.

## Design Review Checklist

- Does the first viewport show a real brand/product/course/customer signal?
- Is there one clear promise, not five slogans?
- Are trust signals concrete and placed before deep detail?
- Does every section have a job?
- Is the CTA placed where the visitor has enough confidence?
- Does mobile preserve the same persuasion sequence?
